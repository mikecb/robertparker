# Testing lookalike detection against real phishing

November 2026. This measured how well `Permute`, `-lookup`, and the `Suspicion` ranking find and rate real phishing domains.

**The main result: generating lookalikes finds very little real phishing.** Of 21,522 phishing domains that name a major brand, `Permute` could generate 18, or 0.1%. Real phishing mostly doesn't look like a typo of the brand. The ranking's signals do point the right way, but the evidence is thin, so the points weren't changed.

## Data

The source is [Phishing.Database](https://github.com/Phishing-Database/Phishing.Database) (MIT license), using `phishing-domains-ACTIVE.txt`, last updated in October 2026: 392,178 hostnames, or 284,378 unique registrable domains. It was only read as a list of names. Nothing on it was visited, and the live part used DNS and RDAP only.

OpenPhish wasn't used, because its terms restrict disclosure and derivative works, and name detection and product development among the restricted uses. PhishTank's terms couldn't be read, so it wasn't used either.

Domains were matched against 55 commonly phished brands, chosen by hand. Matching is by simple rules on the name, so it's approximate.

## What `Permute` can generate

Of the 284,378 domains:

| Group | Domains | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| No brand in the name | 262,854 | 92.43% |
| Brand inside a longer name, like `paypal-merchant[.]ru` | 13,333 | 4.69% |
| Brand only in a subdomain | 6,814 | 2.40% |
| A typo `Permute` doesn't generate, like `metanask[.]cyou` | 1,238 | 0.44% |
| The brand's name on a TLD we don't try | 119 | 0.04% |
| Generated by `Permute`, like `telegramk[.]org` | 18 | 0.01% |

That's 18 of the 21,522 that name a brand. The 92% with no brand in the name is partly a limit of the 55 brands, and many of those are on hosted platforms or compromised sites, whose names say nothing about what they imitate.

### Would generating more help?

Probably not, for three reasons.

**More TLDs.** Only 30% of the 1,256 typos have a label `Permute` generates on some suffix, and those are spread thinly: the top 12 suffixes cover 35% of them, and the top 40 cover 66%. Trying each of about 200 typos on 12 more TLDs is thousands of extra lookups per domain, to reach under 2% of the brand phishing.

**A dictionary.** Adding words to the brand, as dnstwist can, reaches 3.3% of the longer names with the top 25 words, 9.4% with the top 100, and 15.5% with the top 500. Those figures count a match on any TLD, so what a real search would find is less.

**Hosted platforms.** 6,240 of the 13,333 longer names are under private suffixes like `vercel.app`, `duckdns.org`, `webflow.io`, and `pages.dev`, where anyone can pick a name, and there's no way to enumerate them through DNS.

## How the ranking does

A sample of 368 real phishing domains (all 18 generated ones, 150 typos, and 200 longer names, leaving out private suffixes) was rated using the same signals as `-registration`, and compared with 1,262 ordinary lookalikes of 8 brands, leaving out any on the phishing list. Those aren't known to be harmless, but most are parked or defensive.

| Rating | Phishing (61) | Ordinary (1,262) |
|---|---:|---:|
| High | 15% | 5.5% |
| Medium | 26% | 23% |
| Low | 28% | 71% |
| Unknown | 31% | 1.1% |

| Signal | Phishing (61) | Ordinary (1,262) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Registered in the last year | 18% | 5.5% |
| Can receive mail | 49% | 26% |
| Same registrar as the brand | 5% | 28% |

So the direction is right: real phishing is about three times as likely to rate `high`, and much less likely to rate `low`.

**This is weak evidence, for three reasons:**

- **Almost all of the sample was gone.** 307 of the 368 domains sampled no longer exist, which is why only 61 were rated. Phishing domains are short-lived, and this list goes back years. The 15% has a 95% interval of about 8% to 26%.
- **The survivors are skewed.** A domain that's still up long after being listed may have been re-registered, cleaned up, or wrongly listed. That likely understates the recency signal, since the domains that were new when they were used are the ones that are gone.
- **`high` still misses most of it.** Even taking the numbers at face value, 85% of surviving phishing wasn't rated `high`.

The 31% `unknown` is a result in itself. Those had no registration data to compare, against 1.1% for ordinary lookalikes. The causes weren't broken down, but a TLD with no RDAP server is the usual one, which fits the worry that attackers use TLDs that can't be checked this way.

The points weren't changed, because 61 domains can't support it, and tuning on a skewed sample could make the ranking worse.

## What this means

- **`-lookup` answers a narrow question:** which typos of this brand exist? That's useful for finding squatters and for defensive registration, but it isn't phishing detection, and the docs shouldn't suggest it is.
- **The better design runs the other way.** Rather than guessing names from a brand, take a stream of names that really exist, like newly issued certificates or new registrations, and match them against brands. The rules used to group the list above did that with a few dozen lines, over 392,000 names, and they catch longer names and typos on any TLD. It works for hostnames too, so it covers the subdomain group, and hosted platforms if the stream includes them.
- **The ranking needs a fresher test.** Measuring it properly needs domains while they're still live, which means collecting them over time from a recent feed.

## Limits

The brand list is hand-picked, and the name matching has false matches, like `app.link` for `apple` and `godaddysites.com` for `godaddy`. A typo-sized edit distance matches some unrelated short names. Feeds lean towards a few big brands and what's already been reported, and domains that are registered but never used wouldn't appear at all. The figures show direction, not exact rates.
